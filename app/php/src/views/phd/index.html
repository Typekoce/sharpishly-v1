7-Stage PhD Application Lifecycle

A structured roadmap covering the full journey from research niche to enrolment.

1
Months 12–10 before start

Defining Your Research Niche & Route

Decide between STEM lab-based advertised projects (CDTs / Doctoral Focal Awards) and Arts, Humanities or Social Science self-proposed theses (Doctoral Landscape Awards). Clarify whether you need a fully written proposal or a shorter personal statement.

2
Months 10–8 before start

Identifying & Contacting Potential Supervisors

Search departmental profiles, read recent papers, and send a concise, tailored cold-email pitch. Secure informal interest before formal application where possible.

3
Months 9–7 before start

Drafting the Research Proposal / Statement of Purpose

Structure: Title, Research Questions, Literature Gap, Methodology, Ethical Considerations, Timeline (Gantt), and Bibliography. Typical length 1,500–3,000 words for self-proposed projects; shorter statements for many advertised studentships.

4
Months 8–6 before start

Securing Academic References & Transcripts

Request two strong academic references (ideally from Master’s or undergraduate dissertation supervisors), official degree transcripts, and English-language evidence (IELTS/TOEFL) if required.

5
Months 7–5 before start

University Portal Submission & Funding Nominations

Submit via the host university’s postgraduate admissions portal. Ensure separate or linked checkboxes for UKRI studentships, university scholarships or external trusts are correctly selected.

6
Months 5–3 before start

The Academic Panel Interview & Presentation

Prepare a 5–10 minute research pitch, defend methodology choices, answer “Why this supervisor/university?”, and demonstrate resilience and intellectual independence.

7
Months 3–0 before start

Offer Conditions, ATAS Clearance & Visa / Enrolment

Fulfil conditional offers (e.g. pending Master’s Merit/Distinction), obtain ATAS certificates for sensitive STEM subjects, secure Student Visa sponsorship (CAS), and complete induction.

Frequently Asked Questions

Focal Awards (often delivered via Centres for Doctoral Training) are typically cohort-based, themed programmes with structured training and advertised projects. Landscape Awards support individual, often self-proposed, research projects across broader disciplinary landscapes. Both can be funded by UKRI research councils.
For many self-proposed projects it is strongly advisable and sometimes required. For advertised studentships the supervisory team is usually already defined; you still benefit from informal contact. Always check the specific department’s guidance.
Many UKRI awards operate under a cap (commonly around 30% of studentships) for international candidates. Competition is intense. Strong proposals, excellent references and early supervisor engagement are essential. University or external scholarships can sometimes bridge remaining fee gaps.
Approach potential referees at least 6–8 weeks before the earliest deadline. Provide your CV, draft proposal or statement, and a clear list of programmes and deadlines. Follow up politely once.
The Academic Technology Approval Scheme (ATAS) is a UK government clearance required for certain sensitive STEM subjects before a Student Visa can be issued. Your offer letter or university will indicate if ATAS applies. Apply early—processing can take several weeks.

Supervisor Outreach Email Template

Subject: Prospective PhD applicant – [Your research area] – [Intake year]

Dear Dr/Professor [Surname],

I am writing to express my interest in pursuing doctoral research under your supervision, commencing in [October 2027 / January 2028].

I recently completed my [Master’s degree] in [Subject] at [University], where my dissertation examined [brief topic and grade if strong]. I have been following your work on [specific paper or project] and am particularly interested in extending this line of enquiry toward [your proposed angle in 1–2 sentences].

I would value the opportunity to discuss whether there might be scope for supervision (or an advertised project aligned with this interest). I have attached a brief CV and a one-page outline of the proposed research questions and methods.

Thank you for your time. I would be glad to provide any further materials or to arrange a short conversation at your convenience.

Yours sincerely,
[Your full name]
[Email] | [LinkedIn or personal site if relevant]
